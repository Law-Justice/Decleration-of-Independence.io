The Declaration of Independence: Analysis and Reflection

You will show what you understand about the Declaration of Independence and how it compares to our legal system today. There are three ways to do it.

Choose ONE option below. You do not need to do more than one.

Every option asks for the same thinking:

  • An analysis of the Preamble: its core values and how it justifies independence.
  • An analysis of three to five charges against King George III: explain how you see each one addressed in our society today, or how the colonists’ experience influenced it. (In the games you must examine at least three. More is fine but not required.)
  • A reflection (one to two paragraphs) on whether the Declaration is, or isn’t, a reflection of American values. Include quotes, name the core values you see and how they show up in America and our legal system today, and name where the document contradicts the society of its time or ours.

Want to read the whole Declaration first? Read the transcript at the National Archives or watch the video overview. Both options use the same sources.

Pick your option

Option 1: 3D block-world game

Explore a block-built Philadelphia in 1776. Talk to the people who were there, climb parkour trials to reach the charge scrolls, and collect quills.

Choose this if you like games and have a laptop or computer. It is the most demanding option for your device.

Play the 3D game

You submit: your completed essay, a PDF the game gives you at the end.

Option 2: Pixel-art game

Walk a top-down, pixel-art Philadelphia. Talk to the people involved, answer their questions, and collect cards. It runs on almost any device, including phones.

Choose this if you like games but have a slower device, or you want something without jumping.

Play the pixel-art game

You submit: your completed essay, a PDF the game gives you at the end.

Option 3: Written assignment

Watch the video overview, read the primary source, and write your analysis and reflection straight into the Word document. No game needed.

Choose this if you would rather read and write in a document, or you can’t use a game on your device.

Download the written assignment

You submit: your completed document. It opens in Word or Google Docs.

How to turn it in

  1. Choose one option and finish it.
  2. If you played a game: after the final reflection at the end, press Esc (or tap the Satchel button) and open the Case file tab. Choose Download completed essay (PDF). That PDF is your essay, with all of your writing in it.
  3. If you chose the written assignment: complete the document (open it in Google Docs or Word) and save it.
  4. Attach your file to the assignment in Google Classroom and turn it in.

Before you start

  • Stay on one device and one browser. A game saves your progress in the browser you are using. If you switch devices or clear your browsing data, you will start over.
  • Wait until you are finished to download. The essay PDF unlocks only after you complete the final reflection.
  • Games are not a race. Read the sources. They are real quotes, and each one is labeled so you can judge how reliable it is.
  • Platforming too hard in the 3D game? Press Esc, open the Play tab, and turn on Platforming assist. Or switch to Option 2.
  • Wrong choice? You can switch. Each game has a link back to this page on its first screen.

Nothing you type is sent to a server. Your work stays on your device until you attach it in Google Classroom.
